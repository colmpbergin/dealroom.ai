# Section 03 — Market Size

**Company**: BigPawRides **Stage**: Pre-Seed | €100K raise **Date**: 2026-03-21

---

## The Market We Are Building In

BigPawRides addresses a structural gap in urban pet transport: large dog owners
in Dublin who cannot use ride-share services and have no purpose-built
alternative. The market is sized bottom-up from observable demand drivers — trip
occasions, addressable owners, and a validated price point — then cross-checked
against top-down industry data.

---

## Bottom-Up Sizing (Primary)

Bottom-up is the primary method here because the global market data captures
commercial relocation and air freight — categories irrelevant to a local taxi
service. Building from actual trip occasions gives a more credible estimate for
this niche.

### The Funnel

| Step                                                        | Input                            | Output                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Ireland dog population                                      | Licensing data + household proxy | ~700,000 dogs             |
| Dublin share (28.7% of IE population)                       | CSO, Aug 2025                    | ~185,000 dogs             |
| Large breeds (25kg+)                                        | Kennel club + insurance data     | ~64,750 dogs (35%)        |
| Addressable owners (car-free + unsuitable car + preference) | CSO transport data               | ~30,000 households (~52%) |
| Annual trips per addressable owner                          | 10 occasions × 50% conversion    | 5 trips/year              |
| Avg fare                                                    | Business model assumption        | €35                       |

**SAM (Dublin metro) = 30,000 × 5 × €35 = €5.25M/year** Range: €4.9M–€7.5M
(conservative–optimistic)

**TAM (Ireland-wide) = €18M/year** Range: €14.7M–€22.4M

The 10 trip occasions per owner per year are grounded in documented need: 2 vet
visits (WSAVA guidelines), 6 grooming sessions (typical for large double-coated
breeds), and 2 boarding or daycare occasions annually. The 50% conversion to
taxi use accounts for owners who solve the problem another way on any given
trip.

**The most uncertain input is the large-breed percentage (35%).** No
Ireland-specific published survey was accessible. This figure is triangulated
from UK kennel club breed registration data and pet insurance industry
benchmarks, which consistently place large breeds at 30–40% of the insured dog
population in Western markets. A sensitivity range of 25%–45% is used in
scenario analysis.

## Top-Down Cross-Check (Secondary)

The global pet transportation market was valued at **USD 2.4B in 2024**, growing
to USD 5.0B by 2032 at a **9.2% CAGR** (Verified Market Research, 2024).
Applying a Europe share (20%), an Ireland population slice (1.0% of EU), and a
filter for local road transport only yields a Dublin figure of **€0.6M–€0.9M**.

This is substantially below the bottom-up SAM — and that is expected. The global
dataset is dominated by commercial relocation, air freight, and long-haul pet
transport. The local, informal, trip-by-trip taxi market is structurally
undercounted in formal research. The top-down figure represents a conservative
floor; the bottom-up estimate is the more credible primary source for this use
case.

## Serviceable Obtainable Market — Years 1–3

One van, one operator, maximum capacity ~8 trips/day across 5 operating days:

| Year   | Trips/Week | Revenue Range | SOM as % of SAM |
| ------ | ---------- | ------------- | --------------- |
| Year 1 | 15–20      | €27K–€36K     | 0.5–0.7%        |
| Year 2 | 22–30      | €40K–€55K     | 0.8–1.0%        |
| Year 3 | 30–40      | €56K–€75K     | **1.1–1.4%**    |

Year-3 SOM at approximately 1.3% of SAM is well under the 30% threshold at which
SOM claims become implausible. This requires no aggressive market share
assumptions.

**Consistency with financial model**: The financial model targets break-even at
25+ trips/week (~€45K/year) by Month 12–18. That falls within the Year 2 range
above. The market size analysis and the financial model are internally
consistent.

## Market Timing — Why Now

Three structural conditions have converged in 2024–2026:

1. **The COVID dog cohort is in peak demand years.** Dogs adopted in 2020–2022
   are now 3–5 years old — the highest-frequency years for grooming and
   veterinary care. This large urban cohort has created a demand spike that is
   happening now, not in five years.

2. **Ride-share driver policy has hardened.** Uber and Bolt have consolidated
   Dublin's taxi market with app-based accountability systems that make drivers
   systematically more risk-averse about large dogs. This is a structural gap,
   not an occasional inconvenience.

3. **Premium pet spending has crossed a cultural threshold.** Irish pet owners
   now spend more per pet than at any point on record. Euromonitor (May 2025)
   explicitly cites "pet humanization driving demand for premium offerings" as
   the primary growth driver in Irish pet care. The willingness to pay €35–€50
   for a specialist service is established and growing.

## Market Dynamics

- Irish pet care services market growing at **6–9%/year** (Euromonitor,
  May 2025)
- Global pet transport CAGR: **9.2%** (Verified Market Research, 2024)
- Dublin car-free households: ~20–25% of city and rising (CSO commuter data)
- No purpose-built large-dog taxi service identified operating in Dublin as of
  March 2026 — BigPawRides enters an uncontested niche, not a crowded space

## Assumption Confidence Summary

| Assumption                     | Confidence     | Note                                                |
| ------------------------------ | -------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Ireland dog population (~700K) | Medium         | Triangulated from licensing + household data        |
| Large-breed share (35%)        | **Medium-Low** | Most uncertain input; range 25%–45%                 |
| Addressable owners (~52%)      | Medium         | Based on CSO car-free + transport data              |
| Trip occasions (10/year)       | Medium         | Grounded in vet + grooming industry standards       |
| Conversion rate (50%)          | Medium-Low     | No local behavioral data; conservative estimate     |
| Avg fare (€35)                 | High           | Directly from business model; validated by UK comps |

For a €100K pre-seed raise, the market does not need to be €1B. At the
conservative estimate, Ireland alone supports a **€7.5M TAM**. Expanding to
three cities with two vehicles each — the seed-stage thesis — implies a €10–15M
reachable market. That is a viable foundation for the next round.
