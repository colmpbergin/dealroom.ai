## Capital Deployment Timeline

The first €37,000 (vehicle, insurance, and marketing) is deployed in Month 1 to
make operations possible. The founder salary of €2,500/month draws down over 18
months. The €18,000 buffer is held in reserve throughout and is never spent
unless needed — it is the business's safety valve, not an operational line.

## Runway

Under the base case, this raise provides **24+ months of runway**. Revenue
begins in Month 1 and grows steadily through the referral flywheel. The business
reaches break-even at Month 13 (~29 trips/week at €65 average fare), after which
it is self-funding. By Month 18, BigPawRides holds **€61,925 in cash** and
generates **+€1,780/month in positive cash flow** — meaning the runway never
runs out under base case assumptions.

The €18,000 working capital buffer is sized to absorb a stress scenario: demand
ramps 30% slower than projected and one major maintenance event occurs in
Year 1. Even under that scenario, the business reaches break-even by Month 16
before the buffer is exhausted.

---

## The Break-Even Thesis

The capital allocation is designed around a single financial proof point: a
solo-operator, single-vehicle service can reach monthly break-even within 13
months of launch in Dublin. That proof point — not growth at any cost — is what
this round buys. Once demonstrated, it de-risks the seed round and justifies
expansion capital for a second van, a first hire, and entry into Cork or Galway.

---

## Next Round

After achieving break-even and 50+ repeat customers, BigPawRides will raise a
**seed round of €500K–€1M at a €3M–€5M post-money valuation cap**. That capital
will fund:

- Purchase and conversion of a second van
- First driver hire (removes solo-founder key-person dependency)
- Geographic expansion to Cork or Galway
- iOS + Android booking app

The seed trigger metrics — break-even operations and a proven repeat-customer
base — will be live and auditable before any seed conversation begins.
