# Funding Comparables and Valuation Brief — BigPawRides

**Date**: 2026-03-21 **Stage**: Pre-seed SAFE **Target raise**: €100,000
**Geography**: Dublin, Ireland **Sector**: B2C pet services / transport
**Status**: Pre-revenue, idea stage, solo founder

---

## Executive Summary

BigPawRides is raising €100,000 on a pre-seed SAFE to launch a large-dog taxi
service in Dublin. This brief covers valuation comps, Irish funding landscape,
target investor fit, and a recommended valuation framing.

**Key findings:**

1. **The business is fundable, but not by the usual suspects.** Enterprise
   Ireland (HPSU and CSF) and NDRC require high-growth, globally scalable,
   tech-enabled models. BigPawRides — an owner-operated, Dublin-only service
   business — does not qualify for any of these programmes. This is not a
   weakness; it simply focuses the investor target list on angels and consumer
   micro-funds, which is the right channel for this raise.

2. **€100K on a SAFE is appropriate and standard for this stage in Ireland.**
   Irish angels routinely write €10K–€50K cheques; the €100K total is achievable
   from two to five angels. The instrument (SAFE) is increasingly common in
   Ireland and is the right choice.

3. **A valuation cap of €1.5M–€2.5M is the defensible range.** This is lower
   than typical US pre-seed caps because: (a) the business is a service company,
   not a scalable platform; (b) there is zero traction; (c) the Dublin market is
   smaller than a US city; and (d) Irish angels apply a local discount relative
   to US benchmarks. The recommended base case is **€1.8M post-money SAFE cap**.

4. **No direct Irish or EU pet-taxi comps exist at pre-seed.** The closest
   proxies are: (a) pre-seed consumer service businesses in Ireland raising on
   idea-stage validation; (b) early rounds for pet marketplace businesses in the
   EU (DogHero, Pawshake pre-Series A); and (c) US analog (Rover's seed round).
   All point toward the same conclusion: service businesses command meaningful
   discounts to tech platforms at pre-seed.

5. **The highest-probability path to €100K is 3–5 Irish angels via HBAN
   introduction, combined with a direct approach to pet-sector consumer
   angels.** A warm intro through HBAN is the single best lever.

## Part 1 — Pre-Seed SAFE Norms (Ireland and EU)

### 1.1 Global pre-seed benchmarks (2024–2025)

The US benchmark (primarily YC-influenced) sets pre-seed SAFE caps at **$3M–$8M
post-money**, with a median raise of ~$500K. These numbers are driven by the
dominant tech-platform model and the US market size. They are not directly
applicable to a Dublin-based service business.

The UK and broader EU benchmarks are meaningfully lower:

- **UK pre-seed** (SeedLegals data, 2024): raises of £100K–£500K at valuations
  of **£750K–£1.4M** for targets raising £100K–£350K. This implies a pre-money
  in the range of £650K–£1.3M for similarly sized raises.
- **European pre-seed** (Antler research, 2023): €150K–€1M raises are typical;
  valuations range widely based on team strength and scalability, but consumer
  service businesses without a platform component typically land at the lower
  end: **€1M–€3M post-money**.
- **NDRC accelerator** (Ireland): invests €100K per startup via a post-money
  SAFE — the only publicly disclosed Irish pre-seed SAFE benchmark. This
  establishes that €100K / post-money SAFE is a known, accepted instrument in
  the Irish market. NDRC does not disclose its cap but applies a standard
  founder-friendly structure.

### 1.2 The Irish discount

Ireland applies a structural discount to pre-seed valuations relative to US
benchmarks for three reasons:

1. **Smaller exit market.** Irish angel returns are constrained by smaller exit
   multiples. A €1M cap implies a 5x return at a €5M exit — achievable. A $8M
   cap requires a $40M+ exit to produce the same return. The Dublin market for a
   local service business simply does not support high exit multiples.
2. **Limited institutional follow-on.** Unlike US pre-seed rounds that can be
   followed by $3M–$5M seed rounds from micro-VCs, Irish follow-on capital is
   scarcer. Angels price in the risk that the next round may not happen or may
   be smaller.
3. **Service business discount.** Non-platform, non-scalable service businesses
   are valued on discounted cash flow potential plus option value, not on ARR
   multiples. A service business with a fixed capacity ceiling commands a lower
   cap than a SaaS with unlimited seats.

### 1.3 Instrument choice

SAFE is the correct instrument for this raise. Convertible notes (with interest
and maturity dates) are less founder-friendly and create pressure to raise a
priced round within 12–24 months. A post-money SAFE:

- Gives investors certainty on their ownership percentage at the cap
- Has no maturity date or interest accrual
- Is increasingly understood by Irish angels (NDRC has normalised it)
- Is faster and cheaper to execute than a priced round (~€1,500–€3,000 in legal
  fees vs. €15,000+)

**Recommendation**: Use the YC post-money SAFE template. Pair it with a clean
one-page summary of terms. Irish angels who are unfamiliar with SAFEs respond
well to a brief plain-English explanation of how it converts.

### 2.3 What the comps tell us

Three clear signals emerge:

1. **€100K is achievable for a service business at idea stage in Ireland.** It
   is not exceptional, but it is well within the range of what Irish angels
   fund.
2. **A post-money cap of €1.5M–€2.5M is consistent with comps.** Below €1.5M,
   the founder gives up excessive dilution on a small raise. Above €2.5M, the
   cap becomes hard to justify to a rational angel without any traction.
3. **Valuation caps are not disclosed publicly for this profile of raise.**
   Angels in this range negotiate bilaterally. The cap is a negotiating number,
   not a public benchmark. The founder should anchor at €2M and be prepared to
   accept €1.5M.

## Part 3 — Irish Funding Landscape

### 3.1 Enterprise Ireland — HPSU (High Potential Start-Up) Programme

**What it is**: EI's flagship programme for startups with the potential to
develop into significant businesses. Offers up to €1.2M in equity funding via
the Innovative HPSU Fund.

**Eligibility criteria** (key filters):

- Must be in manufacturing or **internationally traded services**
- Must have the potential to create 10+ jobs and €1M+ in export sales within
  three years
- Must demonstrate a scalable, innovative product or service with global market
  potential
- Tech-enabled businesses are strongly preferred

**BigPawRides fit assessment**: **Does not qualify.**

BigPawRides is a Dublin-only, owner-operated service business. It is not
internationally traded, does not have a scalable platform, and cannot credibly
project 10+ jobs or €1M+ export revenue within three years. Even if a platform
pivot were planned, EI requires evidence of the scalable model at application.
Applying to HPSU would be a waste of time and would signal to angels that the
founder does not understand the EI criteria.

**Alternative EI path**: The **Pre-Seed Start Fund** (up to an undisclosed small
amount, typically €50K–€75K for early feasibility) could theoretically apply if
the business had a digital or scalable component. Currently it does not.

---

### 3.2 Enterprise Ireland — Competitive Start Fund (CSF)

**What it is**: Up to €50,000 in equity funding for early-stage startups.

**Eligibility criteria** (key filters):

- Manufacturing or **internationally traded services** (same filter as HPSU)
- Strong interest in climate/green economy and digital innovation
- Must demonstrate global market potential

**BigPawRides fit assessment**: **Does not qualify.**

Same core barrier as HPSU: the business is not internationally traded and is not
scalable in the way EI requires. The €50K amount is also too small to be worth
the application process overhead.

---

### 3.3 NDRC Accelerator

**What it is**: Irish accelerator programme (operated from Dogpatch Labs,
Dublin) that invests €100K per startup via a post-money SAFE. 6-month programme,
12 months of free Dogpatch Labs desk space, 40+ mentors. Approximately 13
startups per year.

**Eligibility criteria** (key filters from NDRC website):

- Must be a "globally scalable, innovative tech startup"
- Portfolio spans education, health, manufacturing, cybersecurity, finance,
  automation — all are tech-enabled
- Strong preference for software/platform businesses

**BigPawRides fit assessment**: **Does not qualify in current form.**

NDRC explicitly targets "globally scalable, innovative tech startups."
BigPawRides has no tech component and is not globally scalable. The NDRC alumni
portfolio (Glitch, Meta-Flux, Nory, DevAlly) are all software or platform
businesses.

**Note**: If BigPawRides were to build a booking platform or marketplace layer,
an NDRC application two to three years from now (once there is traction) is
worth considering. That is a future option, not a current path.

---

### 3.4 Halo Business Angel Network (HBAN)

**What it is**: Ireland's national angel network, a joint initiative of
Enterprise Ireland and InterTradeIreland. Has facilitated €200M+ invested into
700+ companies across 1,000+ angel members. Named Europe's Best Performing
Business Angel Network at the EBAN Congress (June 2025). As of March 2025, had
invested €25M into 82 high-potential startups in one year.

**Sectors HBAN actively backs**: ICT, GreenTech, Fintech, Sports, Mobility,
Medtech, Lifescience, Food and Beverage. All five supported hubs run monthly
showcase events.

**Stated focus**: "Large and growing markets with substantial potential for
value creation." Strong emphasis on "exceptional founders, prioritising startups
with dedicated teams possessing domain expertise."

**BigPawRides fit assessment**: **Partial fit — low-probability but worth a
targeted approach.**

HBAN's stated sector focus does not include pet services or consumer transport.
However, HBAN explicitly assesses each company holistically and its portfolio
includes consumer businesses. The strongest argument for HBAN engagement is the
serial founder background (domain expertise + execution credibility) and the
food and beverage / mobility angle (large dog owners spend heavily; this is a
mobility problem).

**Realistic expectation**: HBAN is unlikely to feature BigPawRides in a
syndicated showcase event. However, individual HBAN angels who are themselves
dog owners or who invest in consumer businesses are worth targeting via direct
outreach. The HBAN network is the best database of active Irish angels.

**Approach**: Request an introductory call with HBAN to discuss the opportunity.
Be transparent that it may not fit their typical mandate. Ask for informal
referrals to individual angels in their network who invest in consumer services.

## Part 5 — Recommended Valuation Framing

### 5.1 The correct methodology for this stage and business type

**Pre-seed, pre-revenue, service business.** ARR multiples do not apply.
Discounted cash flow is theoretically applicable but practically undefensible at
idea stage. The correct methodology is:

1. **Milestone-based valuation**: What milestones does this €100K get the
   business to, and what is the implied step-up in value at the next raise?
2. **Comparable cap negotiation**: What caps are Irish angels paying for similar
   businesses at similar stages? (Derived from comps in Part 2.)
3. **Dilution arithmetic**: What equity does the cap imply, and is that
   acceptable to the founder for the next 3–5 years?
4. **Return math for the angel**: At this cap, what exit size delivers a 5x–10x
   return? Is that exit plausible for this business?

### 5.2 The three scenarios

#### Conservative — €1.5M post-money SAFE cap

- **Implied dilution at €100K raise**: 6.7% (€100K / €1.5M)
- **Angel return at €7.5M exit**: ~5x — the minimum acceptable for most angels
- **Justification**: Reflects the full risk profile: zero traction, solo
  founder, service business (not a platform), no tech moat, Dublin market only.
  This is what a sceptical but fair angel would offer as an opening position.
- **When to accept**: If you need capital urgently, if the lead angel brings
  strong operational value (not just money), or if the angel network is small
  and negotiating leverage is limited.

#### Base — €1.8M post-money SAFE cap (recommended)

- **Implied dilution at €100K raise**: 5.6% (€100K / €1.8M)
- **Angel return at €9M exit**: ~5x; at €18M exit: ~10x
- **Justification**: Reflects the serial founder premium (execution credibility
  meaningfully reduces risk), the validated "Why Now" story (post-COVID dog
  ownership surge, ride-share refusals, urban car-free living), and the
  first-mover positioning in a clearly underserved niche. Supported by UK
  pre-seed comps for a raise of this size.
- **Anchor**: Start conversations at €2M. Settle at €1.8M.

#### Aggressive — €2.5M post-money SAFE cap

- **Implied dilution at €100K raise**: 4.0% (€100K / €2.5M)
- **Angel return at €12.5M exit**: ~5x
- **Justification**: Only defensible if the founder can demonstrate: (a) a
  waitlist of 50+ paying customers or LOIs; (b) a credible platform / franchise
  expansion story that justifies scale; or (c) prior exit(s) that command a
  premium valuation.
- **Risk**: At €2.5M with zero traction, a solo founder, and a service business,
  this cap will be challenged hard by any experienced Irish angel. It risks
  losing the deal entirely.
- **When to use**: After launch, once the first 30–60 days of operations
  demonstrate demand (bookings, repeat rate, NPS). Use this as the post-traction
  cap for a second tranche or a follow-on raise.

### 5.3 What milestones justify a higher cap post-launch

The following milestones would justify a step-up to a €3M–€5M cap for a
follow-on raise (6–12 months post-launch):

| Milestone                                | Rationale                                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **50+ completed trips in first 60 days** | Demonstrates real demand, not just hypothetical |
| **30%+ repeat booking rate**             | Proves loyalty and recurring revenue model      |
| **€2,000–€3,000 MRR**                    | Establishes revenue baseline; implies LTV story |
| **NPS 60+**                              | Service quality evidence; reduces churn risk    |
| **Waitlist of 100+ customers**           | Demand exceeds current supply capacity          |
| **First driver hire / fleet expansion**  | Proves the model is not founder-dependent       |

With these milestones, BigPawRides transitions from "idea-stage service
business" to "early-traction consumer business with proven unit economics" — a
materially different risk profile that commands a higher cap.

### 5.5 The overpricing warning

A cap that doesn't close is worse than a cap that closes at a lower number. For
BigPawRides specifically:

- The Dublin angel market is small. Word travels fast. A cap that gets rejected
  repeatedly becomes known in the community.
- The business has zero traction. Every dollar of cap above €2M requires the
  founder to sell the future, not the present. That is a harder sell for a
  service business than for a tech company.
- A lower cap with a strong angel is better than a higher cap with no one.
  Angels bring introductions, domain knowledge, and credibility — not just cash.

**Do not open at more than €2M. Do not accept less than €1.5M unless the lead
angel brings exceptional non-monetary value.**
