## Financial Model Expectations by Stage

### Pre-Seed Model Requirements

- Format: 1-sheet or max 3 tabs; share as PDF only
- Time horizon: 12–18 months detailed; 3 years directional
- Statements: Cash flow forecast only
- Revenue basis: Assumption-driven (clearly labeled)
- Unit economics: Theoretical with industry benchmarks
- Scenarios: Not required
- Key items: Headcount waterfall, burn rate, runway, use of funds

### Seed Model Requirements

- Format: Multi-tab; share as PDF; full model available on request
- Time horizon: Monthly for 18–36 months; annual for years 3–5
- Statements: P&L + cash flow; balance sheet optional
- Revenue basis: Historical actuals + driver-based projections
- Unit economics: Actuals with trend projections
- Scenarios: Base, best case, worst case required
- Key items: All above + cohort-based retention, sales capacity model

### Universal Financial Rules

- ❌ Never share an interactive Excel model with investors (per Underscore VC)
- ❌ Never use hockey-stick projections without underlying driver logic
- ✅ All assumptions must be explicitly labeled and separable from outputs
- ✅ Numbers must be 100% consistent between deck and model
- ✅ Include a headcount waterfall (role, timing, salary, 25% overhead)
- ✅ Show runway under base + downside scenarios

## The "Why Now" Framework

The most commonly missing element (absent from 75% of decks). Must address at
least one of:

1. **Technology shift** — A new capability now makes this possible (e.g., LLMs,
   mobile, cloud)
2. **Regulatory change** — A law or policy creates new obligation or opportunity
3. **Behavioral shift** — COVID, remote work, demographic change created new
   behavior
4. **Cost inflection** — Something that was expensive is now cheap (compute,
   data, sensors)
5. **Market maturity** — An adjacent market became large enough to support this
   niche
6. **Competitive vacuum** — A major incumbent exited, was acquired, or lost
   focus

## Investor Red Flags Reference

### Immediate Deal-Killers

- Numbers inconsistent between deck and model
- Claiming "no competition"
- Unrealistic hockey-stick projections without driver logic
- Missing IP assignment documentation (common diligence surprise)
- Founders with <50% equity combined at seed stage
- Lying about investor interest or commitments
- Messy or unclear cap table

### Trust Destroyers

- "Our market is a trillion dollars" (no credibility)
- Can't acknowledge uncertainty ("I don't know" is a strength)
- Changing critical data mid-fundraise
- Revenue or traction that can't be verified

### Process Killers

- Data room opened all at once, no staged access
- Outdated documents (signals failed prior fundraise attempts)
- Poor organization (signals how company is run)
- Waiting until term sheet to build data room (starts 65% fewer deals closed)

## Deal Room Structure — Staged Access Model

**Stage 1 — Pre-meeting** (share proactively):

- Teaser deck (6–8 slides via DocSend)

**Stage 2 — Post-first meeting** (share to interested investors):

- Full pitch deck
- Executive summary / one-pager
- Team bios
- Product demo

**Stage 3 — Post-verbal interest / moving toward term sheet**:

- Financial model (as PDF)
- Cap table
- KPI dashboard
- Customer references

**Stage 4 — Due diligence / post-term sheet**:

- Legal documents
- Full financial model (interactive on request)
- Historical financials
- IP documentation
- Material contracts

## Named VC Frameworks

### a16z Core Data Room Requirements (Justine Moore)

Must include: pitch deck, cap table, historical P&L + monthly burn, usage data
with retention cohorts, LTV/CAC with payback

Business-model-specific:

- Marketplaces: GMV, two-sided CAC
- Social: DAU/MAU, D1/D7/D30 retention
- Subscriptions: conversion funnel data
- E-commerce: repeat rates, contribution margin

Exclude (a16z does their own): org charts, detailed 5-year projections, tax
returns, board minutes, market sizing

### Sequoia's 10-Slide Framework

Company Purpose → Problem → Solution → **Why Now** → Market Size → Competition →
Product → Business Model → Team → Financials

### YC's Approach

- Post-money SAFE as default instrument
- Sell ~20% in seed rounds
- "Great engagement and word-of-mouth growth" is the magic signal
- Prioritize good investors over good valuations

### First Round Capital

- "10/90 Rule": different strategies for top-10% vs. other startups
- Demonstrate mastery by showing awareness of AND response to challenges
- Fundraising wisdom: transparency about weaknesses builds more trust than
  hiding them

## VC Due Diligence Checklist (Investor's Perspective)

_Source: The VC Corner — 9 core diligence areas VCs investigate before writing
cheques_

These are the topics a VC's analyst will be checking in your data room. Ensure
every section addresses them.

| Diligence Area                       | What They're Looking For                                                    | Common Deal-Killers                                                   |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Leadership Assessment**            | Founder integrity, execution history, team dynamics, backchannel references | CEO not backable; team has never worked together; no domain expertise |
| **Technology, IP & Product Roadmap** | Codebase quality, scalability, tech stack defensibility, IP ownership       | Missing IP assignments; tech not defensible; roadmap not credible     |
| **Regulatory Strategy**              | Licenses in place, compliance gaps identified, regulatory pathway clear     | No regulatory analysis for regulated industry (fintech, health, data) |
| **Customer Need & GTM Plan**         | Sharp ICP, working channels, real traction vs. vanity metrics               | Vague GTM ("social media and word of mouth"); unvalidated ICP         |
| **Uniqueness & Competition**         | Clear moat, copycat risk assessed, pitch stands out in-market               | Claims no competition; differentiation not defensible                 |
| **Market Size & Opportunity**        | TAM credible (bottom-up), plausible path to 10x investor upside             | TAM top-down only; SOM implying >30% market share                     |
| **Financial Projections & Funding**  | Burn, runway, raise aligned with plan, assumptions credible                 | Hockey stick with no driver logic; raise not tied to milestones       |
| **Exit Strategy**                    | Plausible acquirers, CEO's network assessed, realistic multiples            | No exit analysis; founders haven't thought about it                   |
| **Company Structure & Deal Terms**   | Cap table clean, SAFE/note review, C-Corp status, option pool               | Messy cap table; legacy shareholder issues; not a C-Corp              |

## Data Room Sharing Protocol (from investor advisory research)

**What NOT to include in Tier 1 (non-sensitive / first access):**

- Market size and industry data (investors do their own research — including it
  looks naive)
- Tax returns and audits (only on request, or Tier 3)
- Board meeting minutes (only board decks if asked)
- Detailed resumes (LinkedIn links are more credible than formatted CVs)
- Excessive 5-year financial projections (investors want 12–18mo milestones more
  than far-future forecasts)

**NDA warning:** Requesting an NDA before sharing the data room is a common
mistake that signals distrust and creates friction. Top-tier investors will not
sign NDAs at early stage. Instead, use staged access and only share sensitive IP
post-term-sheet.

**Per-investor customization:** Consider building a dedicated data room for each
serious investor, customized based on your ongoing conversations with them. This
lets you drop in relevant answers to their specific questions and cut access
cleanly if negotiations fall through.

**Collaborative commenting:** Enable viewers to add comments and questions to
documents where possible. This streamlines Q&A and reduces back-and-forth email.
Asynchronous collaboration accelerates diligence.

## Networking & Warm Introduction Statistics

_Source: HBS research on VC deal flow_

| Deal Source                                  | % of Deals |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Former colleagues / work acquaintances of VC | 30%+       |
| Referrals by other investors                 | 20%        |
| Referrals by portfolio companies             | 8%         |
| Cold email pitches from founders             | 10%        |
| VC-initiated contact with entrepreneurs      | ~30%       |

**Key insight:** Only 10% of deals come from cold outreach. Warm introductions
via the VC's network are dramatically more effective. Prioritize building
relationships with VC scouts, associates, and portfolio founders before you need
to raise.

## Gross Margin Benchmarks

_Source: What Investors Look For in a Startup_

- **VC-backable threshold:** 40%+ gross margin
- Sub-40% margins reduce attractiveness for IPO or acquisition
- SaaS typically achieves 60–80% gross margins at scale
- Marketplace models: 30–50% typical
- Hardware/manufacturing: 30–50%+ depending on model
