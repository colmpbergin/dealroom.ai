## The Core Argument (One Paragraph)

Three ride-share platforms dominate Dublin transport. None of them will take a
large dog. The general pet taxis that exist are built for small animals in
carriers. And 63,000 large dogs now live in Dublin — many in apartments, with
owners who have no car — because a pandemic-era adoption surge put record numbers
of large breeds into urban homes that the city's transport system was never
designed to serve. The result: 15,000 Dublin households face a transport gap
every time their dog needs to go anywhere. BigPawRides fills that gap with a
purpose-built van, a founder who is the customer, and a model the UK has already
validated in Bristol and London. €100K gets the van on the road, the first repeat
customers in the book, and the unit economics proven — positioning the business
for a seed round to expand the fleet.

## Raskin's Five Steps Applied

**Step 1 — The Undeniable World Change:**

> The COVID adoption wave put 75,000–100,000 new dogs into Irish homes between
> 2020 and 2022. Many were large breeds. Most landed in Dublin apartments. Those
> dogs are now 3–5 years old — at peak veterinary, grooming, and care needs —
> and the transport system built for humans has no answer for them.

**Step 2 — Winners and Losers:**

Small dog owners won. They put their Chihuahua in a bag, step into any Uber,
any taxi, any bus. Their world works. Large dog owners lost. They adopted during
lockdown, formed bonds, and discovered — when the world re-opened — that every
transport option in Dublin was designed for humans, and that "we don't take
large dogs" is the default answer everywhere they turn.

**Step 3 — The Promised Land:**

A large dog owner in Dublin books a trip the night before. The van arrives. A
loading ramp drops. Their dog boards calmly, travels safely, arrives calm and
on time. The owner gets a WhatsApp photo mid-trip. They pay €35. The logistics
of owning a large dog in a city — the part that has been quietly exhausting —
just disappears.

**Step 4 — Magic Gifts (Capabilities as Enablers):**

- **Purpose-built van**: the right vehicle, crating, ramp, non-slip surfaces —
  so a 45kg Labrador boards as easily as a person, not as a liability
- **Founder-driver model**: the same person every trip; dogs and owners build
  trust with a human, not an app; reliability that a marketplace can never
  guarantee
- **Large dog exclusivity**: not a generalist who "sometimes" takes big dogs —
  a specialist who only does; operators who specialise command premium pricing
  and word-of-mouth that generalists cannot

**Step 5 — Evidence It's Real:**

- PetCabs (Bristol) and PetCars (London) — operating for years, comparable city
  sizes, comparable pricing, profitable. The model is proven. It hasn't crossed
  to Ireland.
- Zero competition in Dublin: Trustpilot, DoneDeal, Gumtree, dogs.ie — no
  large-dog taxi service with a public booking channel exists.
- The founder is the customer: Roland Tritsch's own experience navigating this
  problem in Dublin is the sharpest possible product brief.

## Sawtooth Waves

### Wave 1 — Tactical

**Context (the dip):**
"It's Tuesday morning. Sarah lives in Ranelagh, no car. Her Labrador has his
annual vet check. She books an Uber. Driver arrives, sees a 40kg dog, cancels.
She tries again. Third driver cancels. She calls the vet: 'I can't get there.'
She pays a €50 rebooking fee. The dog doesn't get his checkup."

**Contrast:**
"With BigPawRides, Sarah sends a WhatsApp the night before. The van arrives —
ramp down. Her dog boards calmly. He arrives on time. She goes to work."

**Proof:**
Uber Pet is not available in Ireland. Bolt Dublin lists five service tiers — no
pet category. Free Now has no pet product. Driver discretion means refusal is
the default, not the exception. [Source: bolt.eu/en/cities/dublin, March 2026;
Uber Ireland help pages, March 2026]

**Value level:** Time saved, appointment kept, €50 fee avoided. For one person,
on one morning.

**Context (the dip):**
"Across Dublin, 15,000 large-dog owner households run this same calculation

### Wave 3 — Commercial / Strategic

**Context (the dip):**
"Three ride-share platforms represent the majority of Dublin private hire trips.
None of them will add a large-dog product. Dublin's pet taxi niche is too small
for their product roadmap. General pet taxi operators won't upskill to large
dogs because the van investment is a step change. The gap won't close itself.
It requires someone who builds specifically for it."

**Contrast:**
"First-mover in a niche with compounding recurring demand. Each repeat customer
represents €210–€350 per year. One hundred repeat customers = €25,000 per year
in predictable recurring revenue before any new acquisition. The moat is brand
trust and vet referral relationships — which a copycat cannot buy."

**Proof:**
Global pet transport market: USD 2.4B in 2024, CAGR 9.2% to 2032 (Verified
Market Research). Irish pet care market growing at 6–9% per year; premium pet
services are the fastest-growing subsegment (Euromonitor, May 2025). The market
is growing. The gap is not filling. [Source: Verified Market Research, 2024;
Euromonitor Ireland pet care, May 2025]

**Value level:** The structural case for first-mover advantage. Brand + referral
network = a moat a late entrant cannot close without years of effort.

## Why Now

For six years after Uber launched in Dublin, large dog owners tried and failed to
use the new transport layer the city built for itself. As ride-sharing
consolidated into Uber and Bolt, driver refusals became systematic rather than
occasional — platform algorithms made large dogs invisible to the system. At the
same time, 2020–2022 brought Ireland's largest-ever wave of dog adoption. Those
dogs — now 3–5 years old and settled into Dublin apartments with owners who gave
up cars in a city of BusConnects and bike lanes — are at peak veterinary,
grooming, and boarding demand for the first time. The window is open because a
generation of dog owners has arrived at peak transport need simultaneously, and
the infrastructure for them does not exist. No incumbent will close the gap —
Dublin is too small for ride-share product investment, and the step-change in
capital cost (car to van) is too large for generalist pet taxi operators to
clear. The COVID dogs won't get younger. The transport gap won't close itself.
The moment is now.

## Instructions for Slide Writers

1. **Every slide headline is an assertion, not a label.** "The Problem" is
   wrong. "A 40kg Labrador can't book an Uber" is right. The investor should
   understand the argument from headlines alone.

2. **Lead with the human, not the stat.** Every data point must be anchored to
   a person or a concrete scenario. Introduce the number after the scene — never
   instead of it.

3. **The solution slide describes an outcome, not a product.** "A taxi built for
   large dogs" is a product. "Your dog arrives calm and on time, every time, and
   you didn't have to beg anyone or cancel anything" is the promised land. Lead
   with the outcome.

4. **The competition slide must include "owner drives themselves" as a row.**
   The status quo is the primary competitor. Acknowledging it demonstrates
   intellectual honesty and pre-empts the investor question.

5. **The ask slide must specify the milestones €100K achieves.** Not spend
   categories — milestones. "Break-even on operations by month 18" is a
   milestone. "Vehicle purchase and marketing" is a spend category. Investors
   buy milestones.
