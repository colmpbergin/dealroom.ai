## 1. Investor FAQ

### The 9 Core Questions

**Q: Why will you win?**

BigPawRides is the only purpose-built, large-dog-specific taxi in Dublin. No
other operator uses a purpose-converted van, guarantees large-breed acceptance,
or has invested in the vet-clinic and groomer referral relationships that this
service requires. First-mover positioning combined with vet and groomer referral
relationships and repeat customer loyalty creates a compounding moat that a late
entrant cannot buy quickly. The brand is operationally visible — a liveried van
is a rolling advertisement — and the founder-operator model builds personal trust
with dog owners faster than any platform model.

---

**Q: Why now?**

Three structural conditions have converged in 2024–2026 that did not exist at
this intensity before:

1. The COVID dog cohort (2020–2022) is now 3–5 years old — the highest-demand
   years for professional grooming and veterinary care. This cohort is large,
   urban-heavy, and attached.
2. Ride-share driver policy on large dogs has hardened. Uber and Bolt have
   consolidated Dublin's taxi market; their rating systems now systematically
   discourage large dog transport in a way that did not exist during the
   platform growth years of 2017–2019. Refusals are structural, not occasional.
3. Premium pet service spending has crossed a cultural threshold in urban
   Ireland. The grooming, daycare, and specialist pet food markets have all
   scaled significantly in Dublin between 2020 and 2025, demonstrating revealed
   preference for paying for specialist pet services.

The gap has existed since approximately 2022. No incumbent has moved to fill it.

---

**Q: Why this team?**

Roland Tritsch is a serial entrepreneur and a large dog owner who personally
experienced this problem. He is not inferring the pain from research — he is
the customer. Serial founder track record reduces execution risk meaningfully
compared to a first-time founder attempting to build a service business from
scratch. Founder-market fit at this stage is the single most important risk
factor, and it is fully addressed.

---

**Q: What is the evidence this works?**

Three forms of evidence are available at pre-revenue stage:

1. **Absence of supply:** Zero large-dog taxi services were found on any Dublin
   marketplace, review platform, or app as of March 2026. The gap is documented,
   not assumed.
2. **UK proof of concept:** PetCabs (Bristol, ~470K population) and PetCars
   (London) both operate purpose-focused pet transport services that accept large
   dogs at comparable city scale. The model works commercially. Neither has
   crossed to Ireland.
3. **Founder direct experience:** The founder has personally been refused
   transport for a large dog by Dublin ride-share drivers. This is the sharpest
   possible product brief.

Customer interview data is the acknowledged gap. Ten customer interviews are on
the pre-launch action list. See [Section 7](#7-open-questions-and-pre-launch-action-list).

**Q: What is the exit?**

Three credible paths exist on a 7–10 year horizon:

1. **Acquisition by a pet care platform** — Rover, Wag, or PetBacker seeking
   offline, physical service capability in the EU. BigPawRides would represent
   a proven, operationally excellent beachhead into the Irish market.
2. **Acquisition by a mobility or logistics company** — Bolt or Uber as they
   build premium pet transport tiers; or a logistics company entering the
   premium last-mile consumer category.
3. **Independent multi-city franchise model** — after proving the model in
   Dublin, a scalable playbook for Cork, Galway, and UK cities. This path
   requires the most capital but retains the most equity upside.

No exit path requires BigPawRides to become a technology platform. All three
paths are predicated on proven unit economics and a defensible brand in a
structurally underserved niche.

### Additional Commonly Asked Questions

**Q: What does the company do?**

BigPawRides is Dublin's first purpose-built large-dog taxi. We transport large
dogs (25kg+) in a professionally converted Ford Transit Custom van — to the vet,
to the groomer, to kennels, and back. We exist because large dog owners cannot
reliably use Uber, Bolt, or Free Now, and most do not own a car capable of safely
transporting a 40–60kg dog.

---

**Q: Who are the target customers?**

Three primary ICP segments:

1. **Urban apartment dwellers with large dogs** — car-free or unsuitably-vehicled
   households in Dublin 1, 2, 4, 6, 8. Approximately 22% of Dublin households
   have no car. This segment has no alternative.
2. **Dual-income households where the dog needs transport mid-week** — owners
   with suitable cars but unavailable to drive on a Tuesday morning for a
   10am vet appointment. The dog needs to go; they cannot take it.
3. **High-spend pet owners who prefer professional transport** — senior dog
   owners, anxious handlers, or owners whose dogs are recovering from surgery
   and need careful, specialist handling. Willingness to pay is highest here.

---

**Q: What are the barriers to entry for a copycat?**

The moat is operational and reputational, not technical:

- **Brand recognition:** Every week of operations adds Google reviews, Facebook
  group mentions, and Instagram posts that a new entrant cannot replicate.
- **Vet and groomer referral relationships:** A vet clinic that already
  recommends BigPawRides will not switch to a new entrant without strong reason.
  These relationships are earned, not bought.
- **Dog-owner loyalty:** Large dog owners are anxious about handing their dog
  to a stranger. Once they trust the driver, they do not switch.
- **Founder-operator trust:** The same person drives every trip. The dog knows
  the driver. This is qualitatively different from a marketplace model.

A copycat can buy a van. They cannot buy the reviews, the relationships, or the
trust that compounds over 12–24 months of operations.

---

**Q: What IP does the company have?**

BigPawRides is a service business. There is no patentable technology. The
defensible assets are brand (name, livery, domain, social handles), customer
relationships, and operational know-how. The company name and brand elements
will be registered as trademarks at launch.

---

**Q: How will you use social media and marketing?**

Primary channel: referral via Dublin vet clinics and grooming salons. This is
a zero-cost channel with high conversion — a vet receptionist recommending
BigPawRides to a client who just asked "how do I get Biscuit home after
surgery?" is a higher-intent referral than any digital ad.

Secondary channels: Facebook and WhatsApp groups for Dublin large dog owners
(identifiable communities with high engagement), Google My Business (search
intent), and Instagram (visual content — a liveried van + happy Wolfhound is
inherently shareable). Paid media budget is €100/month, intentionally minimal
at launch.

---

**Q: What is the pricing model?**

Per-trip pricing: €25–€50 depending on trip distance and complexity. Blended
average of €35 is used in all financial modelling. No subscription at launch.
The recurring revenue dynamic is behavioural — regular grooming customers book
every 6–8 weeks without a formal subscription structure.

---

**Q: What percentage of stock is set aside for employees?**

No option pool has been created at pre-seed stage. A standard 10–15% option
pool will be created at the seed round for the first driver hire and any future
key hires.

---

**Q: What are the factors that limit faster growth?**

Single operator, single vehicle. Physical capacity ceiling: ~8 trips/day,
~5 days/week = ~40 trips/week maximum. The financial model's base case reaches
39 trips/week by Month 18, which is near capacity. Growth beyond that point
requires a second vehicle and driver — the seed round thesis.

## 2. Extended Competitive Analysis

### Summary Finding

BigPawRides has no direct, dedicated competition in the Dublin large-dog taxi
segment as of March 2026. The landscape divides into three tiers: inactive
or generic local operators, ride-share platforms with no pet product in Ireland,
and the status quo (owners driving themselves). UK operators (PetCabs, PetCars)
prove the model commercially but have not moved to Ireland.

Full detail is in [research/competitors/competitor-brief-2026-03-21.md][comp-brief].

### Competitor Notes

**PetTaxi.ie** — The domain does not resolve as of March 2026. No Trustpilot
listing, no Google Maps presence confirmed. Either dormant or never fully
launched. Functionally no competition; a minor risk if they reactivate once the
niche is proven.

**Generic Dublin micro-operators** — No named, bookable pet taxi service in
Dublin appeared on Trustpilot, Google Maps, or any Irish media source found
during research. The segment is informal, car-based, small-dog-focused, and
digitally invisible.

**PetBacker.ie** — A global peer-to-peer marketplace operating in Ireland.
Searching for Dublin pet taxi returns a request-and-quote flow with no
pre-listed providers. Provider density is low to zero for the taxi category in
Dublin specifically. The platform's generalist carer model cannot deliver
guaranteed large-dog capability.

**Uber and Bolt** — Neither operates a dedicated pet product in Ireland. Uber
Pet has not launched in Ireland as of March 2026. Both platforms operate on
driver discretion for pets; large dog refusals are systematic. A Dublin Uber
Pet launch is unlikely within 3–5 years: the market is too small to justify a
dedicated product tier.

**Free Now** — Operates with traditional licensed taxi drivers in Dublin.
Structurally worse than Uber/Bolt for large dogs: saloon cars, self-employed
drivers who are responsible for interior cleaning, and no platform incentive
to accept large animals.

**Status quo (owner drives)** — The dominant solution. Fails when: (1) no car
or unsuitable car (22%+ of Dublin households); (2) owner unavailable mid-week;
(3) trip distance or complexity makes personal driving impractical. The cost
of the status quo is not zero — it includes missed appointments, rebooking fees,
and social capital spent asking family members for help.

**PetCabs / PetCars (UK)** — Closest structural analogues. Estate-car based,
purpose-focused, proven at comparable city scale. Neither has a Dublin or
Ireland presence. Their estate-car limitation (no purpose-built loading ramp,
no temperature control, no crate anchoring) is the product gap BigPawRides
fills.

## 3. Market Research Methodology

### Overview

The TAM/SAM/SOM analysis was built bottom-up from first principles using Irish
population data, dog ownership estimates, breed size distributions, and trip
occasion frequencies. A top-down cross-check from global market data was used
as a directional floor, not a primary estimate.

### Bottom-Up Methodology (Primary)

**Step 1 — Ireland dog population:** 400,000–450,000 licensed dogs per year
(Department of Housing dog control statistics). Applying a 1.6–2.0x multiplier
for the unlicensed population (licensing compliance estimated at 50–60%) yields
650,000–900,000 total dogs. Central working estimate: 700,000.

**Step 2 — Dublin share:** Dublin City and County accounts for 28.7% of
Ireland's population (CSO, August 2025). Applying population share plus a 10%
urban downward adjustment for lower dog ownership density in apartment-heavy
areas: ~185,000 dogs in Dublin metro.

**Step 3 — Large breed share (25kg+):** Triangulated from AKC/Kennel Club breed
registration proxies and veterinary insurance data. Working estimate: 35% of
Dublin dogs are large breeds. Confidence: medium-low. Sensitivity range: 25–45%.

**Step 4 — Addressable owners:** Applied three filters — car-free households
(22%), households with a car unsuitable for large dog transport (20%), and
households that prefer professional transport even with a car (10%). Total
addressable: ~30,000 large dog owner households in Dublin.

**Step 5 — Annual trips:** 10 raw trip occasions per dog per year (2 vet, 6
grooming, 2 boarding/kennel). 50% conversion to taxi use. Effective: 5 trips
per addressable owner per year.

**Step 6 — TAM/SAM calculation:**

| Level | Definition | Estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TAM | All large-dog transport in Ireland | €14.7M–€22.4M/year (central: €18M) |
| SAM | Dublin metro large-dog transport | €4.9M–€7.5M/year (central: €5.25M) |
| SOM Year 3 | One-vehicle operator capture | ~€65,000–€75,000/year (1.3% of SAM) |

The SOM is well below the 30% threshold at which investor credibility becomes
strained. It is internally consistent with the financial model's break-even
target of 25+ trips/week.

### Top-Down Cross-Check (Secondary)

Global pet transportation market: USD 2.4 billion (2024), growing at 9.2%
CAGR to USD 5.01 billion by 2032 (Verified Market Research, 2024). Applying
Europe's share (20%), Ireland's population share (1.0%), and a 50% filter for
local ground transport yields a Dublin-specific estimate of €0.6M–€0.9M.

This figure is lower than the bottom-up SAM. The discrepancy is expected: the
global dataset primarily captures commercial relocation and pet cargo services,
which are structurally distinct from the local trip-by-trip taxi market
modelled here. The local market is largely informal and cash-based in most
countries, and is structurally undercounted in formal market research.

**Conclusion:** Bottom-up is the primary estimate. Top-down establishes a
conservative floor.

### Data Confidence

| Input | Confidence | Gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ireland dog population | Medium | Licensing compliance estimate is approximate |
| Dublin population share | High | CSO data (August 2025) |
| Large breed share (35%) | Medium-Low | No Ireland-specific survey accessible |

## 4. Vehicle and Equipment Research

### Hypothesis Tested

That a Ford Transit Custom-sized van can be converted into a purpose-built
large-dog transport vehicle within a €30,000–€40,000 total budget (base vehicle
plus conversion).

### Verdict: VALID. Confidence: High.

### Key Findings

**No dedicated Irish pet transport van conversion specialists were found.** The
Republic of Ireland's vehicle conversion industry serves commercial and utility
purposes (refrigeration, equine transport, welfare vehicles). No Irish company
with a visible pet transport specialism was identified.

**The UK K9 conversion industry is mature.** Multiple suppliers have proven
Transit Custom-compatible products:

| Company | Specialism | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Halls Vehicle Conversions | K9/police dog transport | Transit Custom; aluminium units with handler bulkhead |
| Pro-Tect Aluminium | Custom dog transport boxes | Bespoke aluminium; sold to working dog professionals |
| DT Boxes | Dog transport boxes (fibreglass/aluminium) | Purpose-built units; fit multiple van sizes |
| SRV Cages / Van Vault | Modular cage systems | No permanent conversion needed; removable |

**Cost ranges:**

| Option | Low | High | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Used Transit Custom + full K9 conversion | €20,000 | €32,000 | Best balance of quality and cost |
| Used Transit Custom + modular cage | €17,000 | €27,000 | Lowest-risk first vehicle; easier to resell |
| Pre-converted ex-fleet K9 van (UK) | €12,000 | €20,000 | Best value; purpose-built, proven; add VRT |
| New Transit Custom + full conversion | €36,000 | €48,000 | May exceed budget at top end |

The financial model uses €28,000 (used Transit Custom ~€22K + K9 conversion
~€6K). This is consistent with the research range.

**VRT note:** UK-sourced vehicles registered in Ireland attract Vehicle
Registration Tax. Budget €1,500–€4,000 additional depending on vehicle age
and value.

**Standard conversion features:** Aluminium or fibreglass transport compartment;
non-slip rubber or chequerplate flooring; removable floor and drainage channels
for cleaning; roof vent or forced-air fan; side or rear ramp entry; built-in
crash-rated anchor points; handler bulkhead.

### Recommended Next Action

Obtain three direct quotes from UK K9 conversion companies (Halls Vehicle
Conversions, DT Boxes, Pro-Tect Aluminium) for a Transit Custom-compatible
dog transport unit. Also search "ex-police K9 van for sale" on AutoTrader UK
and eBay UK for pre-converted ex-fleet units.
