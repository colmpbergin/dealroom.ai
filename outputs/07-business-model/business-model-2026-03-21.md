# Section 07 — Business Model

**BigPawRides** | Pre-Seed | €100K Raise | Updated: 2026-03-21

## Revenue Model

BigPawRides operates a **transactional per-trip model** — no subscription, no
platform fee, no driver commission. Revenue is 100% captured by the
owner-operator on every booking.

| Trip Type                            | Price        |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------ |
| Short city trip (<5 km)              | €25          |
| Standard trip (5–15 km)              | €35–€40      |
| Long trip / vet specialist / airport | €50+         |
| **Blended average**                  | **€35/trip** |

Pricing is set below comparable UK services. [UK Pet Taxi][ukpettaxi] charges
£70–80 for equivalent trips, suggesting meaningful upside to raise rates as
brand and demand mature. This is a deliberate early-market decision — not a
ceiling.

## Unit Economics

### Per-Trip Economics

| Item                           | Amount           |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------- |
| Revenue per trip (blended avg) | €35.00           |
| Variable cost per trip (fuel)  | €2.75            |
| **Gross margin per trip**      | **€32.25 (92%)** |

Fuel is the only meaningful variable cost. All other costs — van insurance,
vehicle finance, founder salary — are fixed, which means margin expands directly
with volume once fixed costs are covered.

At 100 trips/month, allocating the founder salary across the trip base produces
a **contribution margin of ~€3.75/trip**. This tightness at the floor is the
point: every trip above break-even drops almost entirely to the bottom line.

### Break-Even Analysis

| Item                                                            | Monthly              |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Fixed costs (salary + insurance + vehicle finance + fuel floor) | ~€3,465              |
| Break-even trip volume                                          | ~99 trips (~23/week) |
| Break-even timeline (projected)                                 | Month 13             |

Break-even is not a stretch target — it is 23 booked trips per week in a city of
1.4 million people where no direct competitor operates. The sole structural
requirement is consistent demand-generation, not cost reduction.

## Customer LTV and CAC

### Lifetime Value

Large-dog owners have recurring, calendar-driven transport needs:

- Grooming: every 6–8 weeks (~7–8 trips/year)
- Vet appointments: 2x/year
- Boarding drop-off and collection: 2–4x/year

| LTV Driver                       | Assumption               |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Trips per customer per year      | 7 (conservative)         |
| Revenue per trip (blended)       | €35                      |
| Annual LTV                       | **€245/dog**             |
| Customer lifespan (dog lifespan) | 5–10 years               |
| 5-year LTV                       | **~€1,225 per customer** |

The dog lifespan framing matters: a customer acquired at year one of a dog's
life is likely a customer for a decade. Loyalty in this category is structurally
high — owners find a provider they trust and stay.

### Customer Acquisition Cost

| Channel                                          | Estimated CAC |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------- |
| Vet clinic referrals (cards + relationship time) | €0–€30        |
| Grooming studio referrals (reciprocal model)     | €0–€5         |
| Facebook / WhatsApp large-dog owner groups       | €0–€15        |
| Word of mouth                                    | €0 marginal   |
| **Blended estimated CAC**                        | **<€30**      |

All primary channels are relationship-based and organic. There is no dependency
on paid acquisition at launch.

### LTV:CAC Ratio

| Metric      | BigPawRides | VC Benchmark (3:1) |
| ----------- | ----------- | ------------------ |
| 5-year LTV  | €1,225      | —                  |
| Blended CAC | <€30        | —                  |
| **LTV:CAC** | **>40:1**   | 3:1 minimum        |

This ratio is pre-revenue and based on conservative assumptions. Even at a CAC
of €100 — three times the high-end estimate — the ratio remains above 12:1
against the 3:1 VC benchmark.

## Why This Model Works

**Recurring demand is structural, not assumed.** Grooming, vet visits, and
boarding are not discretionary. They recur on a fixed cadence regardless of the
economy. This is closer to a utility than a lifestyle service.

**No platform dependency.** The owner-operator model eliminates driver
acquisition cost, commission loss, and the retention problem that kills
marketplace unit economics. Revenue is 100% retained.

**Fixed-cost structure creates operating leverage.** Salary, insurance, and
vehicle costs are largely fixed. Beyond break-even, every additional trip at €35
requires only ~€2.75 in incremental cost. At 150 trips/month (50 above
break-even), incremental revenue of €1,750 costs less than €140 to deliver.

**Pricing is below market.** UK comparables charge 2–2.3x the BigPawRides
blended rate. This is a conscious go-to-market choice, not a constraint. Price
increases are available as a lever once the brand is established.

## Revenue Growth Path

| Phase              | Trigger                            | Volume            | Mechanism                                               |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Phase 1 — Pre-seed | This raise                         | 1 van, 1 operator | Sole founder; Dublin city; break-even at ~25 trips/week |
| Phase 2 — Seed     | Demonstrated break-even + waitlist | 2 vans, 2 drivers | Hire 2nd driver; 50–80 trips/week combined              |
| Phase 3 — Growth   | Unit economics proven at scale     | Multi-city        | Cork, Galway replication; franchising model viable      |

Franchise potential is a Phase 3 option, not a Phase 1 claim. It is noted here
because the owner-operator playbook is fully replicable — a franchisee receives
the van spec, the referral network playbook, and the booking system. The unit
economics are transparent and verifiable.
