## 🔴 Deal-Killers — Fix Before Sending Anything

**1. Company not incorporated.**
`context/legal-readiness.md` is entirely unfilled. No entity type, no incorporation date, no registered agent. An investor who asks "are you incorporated?" has no answer. No SAFE can be executed without a legal entity.
*Action: Incorporate in Ireland (Limited Company) or engage a startup solicitor immediately. This is the single highest-priority action in the entire data room.*

**2. No founder agreements, IP assignment, or vesting schedule.**
Even for a solo founder, IP assignment (assigning any prior work to the company) and a vesting schedule are required before taking investment. Missing these is a standard diligence deal-killer.
*Action: Engage solicitor to set up founder agreement, IP assignment, and 4-year/1-year cliff vesting before any investor conversation.*

**3. Cap table is empty.**
No capitalization management tool, no share structure, no option pool. A SAFE investor needs to know what they're buying into.
*Action: Set up Carta or a simple spreadsheet cap table. Define share classes, founder shares, and planned option pool size.*

## 🟡 Important Gaps — Fix Before Investor Meetings

**4. SAFE cap not formally set.**
€1.8M is the recommendation from research/financials/funding-brief-2026-03-21.md. It has not been confirmed with a solicitor or formally adopted. Investors will ask what the cap is — there needs to be a definitive answer.
*Action: Confirm €1.8M cap with solicitor. Update context/fundraising-stage.md with the confirmed cap.*

**5. Commercial pet transport licensing not confirmed.**
Research flagged this as an open question: no Ireland-specific licensing requirement was found, but it wasn't definitively cleared either. An investor will ask "are you legally allowed to do this?"
*Action: Engage Irish solicitor or contact NTA/DAFM directly before any investor meeting.*

**6. No verbatim customer quotes.**
All customer voice quotes in Section 02 are composites reconstructed from research patterns — not verbatim from real Dublin large dog owners. This is disclosed, but a real quote is materially stronger.
*Action: Post in Dublin dog owner Facebook groups this week. Ask one question: "How do you get your large dog to the vet/groomer?" Screenshot 3 responses. This takes 30 minutes and dramatically strengthens the deck.*

**7. Insurance cost is low-confidence.**
The €450/month insurance assumption is flagged as low-confidence in the financial model. If the actual cost is significantly higher (e.g., €800–€1,200/month for specialist pet transport), the break-even month moves out materially.
*Action: Obtain 3 specialist insurance quotes before finalising the financial model and sharing with investors.*

**8. Exit story not in the main deck.**
The exit paths (acquisition by Rover/Wag/mobility platform; franchise model) are in the Appendix FAQ but not in the executive summary or investment memo.
*Action: Add 2 sentences to Section 13 (Investment Memo) and Section 01 (Executive Summary): "Plausible exits: acquisition by a pet services platform (Rover, PetBacker) or mobility company (Bolt, Uber) seeking offline capability; or a multi-city franchise model with a 7–10 year horizon."*
